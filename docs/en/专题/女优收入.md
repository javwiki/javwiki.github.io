# AV Actresses' Income

Income can mean a producer's performance fee, the performer's allocation from an agency, the actual bank payment, or disposable income after costs and taxes. Interviews and recruitment advertisements often reveal only one component and cannot simply be combined into an industry salary table.

## Distinguishing the figures

| Figure | Meaning | What needs checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fee per title | Payment for a performance | Agency total or personal allocation; other work included |
| Personal allocation | Contractual performer share | Calculation base, percentage and separate charges |
| Monthly or annual income | Receipts over a period | Jobs, income sources, tax treatment and gaps in work |
| Bank payment | Money actually received | Withholding and other deductions |
| Product revenue | Sales from products or business | Production, logistics, platform costs and profit |

Per-title, daily, monthly and annual figures are different units. Currency conversion also needs a date and exchange rate.

## What verifiable numbers establish

In a personal essay, Suzumi Suzuki recalled a debut fee of **¥1 million**. Originally published on **29 December 2017** and republished in 2020, it describes her earlier experience, rather than a standard rate for 2020 or today. [Suzuki's essay](https://bunshun.jp/articles/-/30064)

A **2021** anonymous Bunshun interview records a respondent describing a **¥400,000** debut fee, later fees around **¥100,000** per title, and AV income around **¥300,000** monthly at that time. This is individual testimony without auditable payment records. The headline's **¥900,000** includes other work, rather than AV income alone. [Individual income interview](https://bunshun.jp/articles/-/49872)

Agency ACT's recruitment page describes a **50%–70%** performer allocation depending on support, recorded in the contract. This is the company's stated arrangement in commercially motivated recruitment material, not an industry-wide percentage or an earnings guarantee. [ACT's payment explanation](https://actenter.com/fee/)

These figures differ in source, period and definition. They cannot legitimately be averaged or used to rank performers' incomes.

## Why fees differ

ACT lists performance category, filming content and sales as factors. Its recruitment estimates for solo and other categories are not universal industry rates. [ACT's work and payment page](https://actenter.com/fee/)

Analysis must separate the fee per job from the number of jobs actually obtained. Exclusive-contract schedules, permission to accept other work and guaranteed payments depend on the terms. Category labels alone do not establish monthly earnings, nor does a debut fee establish later rates.

Multiplying a high payment by twelve assumes identical monthly work and terms; it does not establish realised annual income.

## From contractual fee to disposable income

Records can be checked in this order: **contract amount → personal allocation → payment after deductions → personal costs and final tax liability**. Costs already reflected in an allocation must not be deducted twice, and producer or agency expenses are not automatically personal expenses.

Japan's National Tax Agency includes film performance payments among categories subject to withholding, with treatment depending on the recipient, payer and payment. Withholding at payment is not the same as final annual tax liability. This article does not apply one flat tax rate to every performer's earnings. [Tax Agency: covered payments](https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/gensen/2792.htm), [withholding obligations](https://www.nta.go.jp/taxes/shiraberu/taxanswer/gensen/2793.htm)

## Other work and older titles

Events, photography sessions, photo publications, sponsorships, content and brands each require checking payment terms. Yua Mikami's official website presents her clothing brand and public activities, demonstrating work beyond AV without establishing auditable personal annual earnings. [Mikami's official website](https://miss.co.jp/)

Continued sales of older titles do not automatically establish royalties or revenue sharing. Further payment and its recipient depend on the contract. Sales rankings measure titles or platform activity, rather than performers' bank receipts.

## Limits on averages and rankings

The cited examples and recruitment material offer no representative industry sample. They cannot establish mean or median income or a ranking of the highest earners. Celebrity interviews, high-income recruitment examples and anonymous accounts each involve selection bias.

Online earnings claims require a speaker, date, income definition, period and treatment of taxes and costs. Claims of hundreds of millions of yen annually or universal fee tables lacking those details remain unverified.
