# The Koyoi Konan controversy

In September 2025, Koyoi Konan, a former AV actress now working as a DJ and YouTuber, was accused of concealing her marriage and, through an event company whose representative was her spouse, producing and selling other actresses' merchandise without authorization, as well as failing to pay fan-club revenue. The allegations were made publicly by influencer Takizawa Gareso, actress Nanase Alice and ami (formerly Ami Tokita). Koyoi's side denied the claims through an account described as her staff, calling them "entirely baseless" and reportedly considering legal action.

"Enjō" is a Japanese internet term for a sudden surge of criticism directed at a person or organization. This page sets out the public timeline and distinguishes allegations, responses and claims that still lack verifiable material. **It covers the September 2025 dispute over money and merchandise; earlier health-related events and her retirement decision are not treated as evidence in this case.**

## Background

Koyoi debuted in May 2021 as an S1 exclusive actress and announced her retirement on YouTube on 1 February 2025. [TVBS reported on 2 February 2025](https://news.tvbs.com.tw/entertainment/variety/2766850) that in the retirement video she cited TikTok's suspension of her account, the loss of an account with about 400,000 followers and her agency's failure to respond promptly as reasons for leaving. [The retirement video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miQ2reIXCkk) remains the primary source for her own account.

That background matters because **after an AV actress retires, fan clubs, events, merchandise and gravure often shape her income more directly than films do.** These operations are usually outsourced to companies and platforms, and involve likeness rights, merchandise licensing, revenue shares and transparent accounting. When one company controls event planning, photo material and fan-club payments, the performer can easily lose control of both merchandise and income without knowing it.

## The parties' positions

### Nanase Alice

In her 20 September statement, Nanase said she worked with the fan-club and event company after Koyoi introduced her. During that work, merchandise was produced and sold without permission and payments went unpaid, after which she could not reach the company. She said Koyoi told her directly by DM that the matter was unrelated to her, so she checked the company registry, reported to her agency and consulted a lawyer before issuing the warning. She said "three people and one company" had been harmed "within the range I have confirmed" and that she wanted to prevent new victims.

On 21 September she revised the figure to "three people and two companies", said others had been approached, and said another company was pursuing a criminal case. She said she had sent a content-certified letter and stressed that she was not looking for a fight, but wanted the company and Koyoi to reconsider legal and ethical boundaries. Responding to the claim that she had staged the dispute, she said that although fan-club rights and management had been entrusted to her, the receiving account was not hers, so there was no reason to stage anything. ([Erochika's roundup](https://ertk.net/archives/998308.html) quotes the post.)

### ami (formerly Ami Tokita)

In an Instagram "report", ami said she had outsourced her fan-club operations to iS Inc. (written "IS" in Nanase's post), which Koyoi introduced, and that its representative was said to be Koyoi's spouse. She said the company provided no statements or receipts and used false explanations to conceal that part of the revenue had been misappropriated. Merchandise was also produced and sold without authorization, and the proceeds were not paid, she said. When she contacted the operations manager, he stopped responding; the head office also did not reply, so she concluded that the company could not be trusted. She accused Koyoi and her husband of hiding their marriage and "deceiving many people", and said she would now run the fan club herself. ([coki, 6 October 2025](https://coki.jp/article/column/60431/))

### Takizawa Gareso

On 20 September, Takizawa said he had received several similar DMs. They alleged that Koyoi approached Saika Kawakita and Nanase Alice under the pretext of a social-media collaboration, then introduced an event company whose representative was her spouse, and that the company produced and sold Kawakita's "worn underwear" and Nanase's merchandise without permission. He initially said he would wait until his promotional sale of Koyoi's and Nanase's work ended. Later that day, in a post quoted by [Erochika's roundup](https://ertk.net/archives/998308.html), he said Koyoi herself was a director of her spouse's company, calling it "effectively a joint plan by the couple". These are Takizawa's hearsay and interpretation; this page has not obtained corporate registry records or confirmation from Koyoi.

On 5 October, Takizawa said ami had also alleged victimization. He said he asked Koyoi directly about her spouse and was told "I don't know", which he regarded as evasion. ([Takizawa's post](https://x.com/tkzwgrs/status/1974790540836409626))

### Koyoi Konan's side

As of this update, Koyoi has not published a point-by-point response or apology. zakⅡ reported that an account claiming to be Koyoi's staff sent Takizawa a DM calling the claims "entirely baseless", demanding deletion and saying legal action was being considered. The article presents Nanase's allegations and Koyoi's denial side by side. ([zakⅡ, 20 September 2025](https://www.zakzak.co.jp/article/20250920-EDJQCUW63VG4PLZ5LVAYS4YPZM/))

In the public material reviewed for this page, Koyoi has not confirmed her marriage. She continued updating YouTube and fan-club content during the dispute; some users saw the "deny but do not explain" approach as defiant, and it contributed to the spread of the firestorm.

### Saika Kawakita

Takizawa named Saika Kawakita (河北彩花, then using the name 河北彩伽) as one of the actresses approached and said her "worn underwear" was sold without permission. In the public material reviewed for this page, however, neither Kawakita nor her agency has confirmed the matter or given a detailed account. **"Kawakita is a victim" is therefore currently an allegation, not an established fact.**

## Why it became a firestorm

- **A sharp contrast in status.** Koyoi was a popular S1 exclusive actress and, after retiring, built a YouTube, DJ and fan-club presence around her "divine bust" image. A story about a hidden marriage, an affiliated company and money sharply reversed that image, which fed the spread.
- **The allegations were specific and involved more than one person.** This was not an abstract complaint about character but a named business dispute over merchandise, fan-club revenue, unpaid bills and lost contact. Nanase and ami came forward in succession, turning a "quarrel between two women" into a "multiple victims" narrative.
- **Nanase, one of the accusers, had a lawyer and her agency behind her.** She said she had consulted her agency and a lawyer and sent a content-certified letter. That is easier to trust than an anonymous leak.
- **The amplifier's reach.** Takizawa has millions of followers. He first teased the story by saying he would wait until a sale ended, then added the director claim and ami's allegations, keeping the topic supplied with new material.
- **The response.** Koyoi's side denied the claims through a staff DM, demanded deletion and mentioned legal action, but did not publicly explain the contracts, accounts or marriage. Continuing to update paid content at the height of the controversy was easily read as a refusal to take responsibility.
- **Cross-border spread.** Koyoi has a steady following in Taiwan, Hong Kong and the Chinese-speaking world. Once Chinese-language outlets reported the story as "小宵虎南", it moved quickly onto Chinese-language social platforms; headlines about a secret marriage, "scamming peers" and "pocketing the money" added to the drama.
- **A structural industry problem.** Fan clubs, events, merchandise and photo material are often bundled into one small company, and performers do not necessarily understand the contracts or accounts. When the same company controls both the money and the material, a dispute becomes hard to untangle after the fact.

## Evidence boundaries and how to read this page

- **"Fraud" and "embezzlement" are the accusers' characterizations, not court findings.** As of October 2026, this page has found no public criminal judgment, civil judgment or settlement. Nanase's and ami's accounts are allegations; Koyoi's denial should be recorded alongside them.
- **Koyoi has not publicly confirmed the marriage.** The claims that she is married and that her husband runs the company come mainly from Takizawa's reporting and ami's statement. This page has not obtained a family register, marriage certificate or confirmation from Koyoi.
- **Kawakita's position requires the most caution.** She was named only in Takizawa's account and has not publicly confirmed it. Being named is not the same as confirmed victimization.
- **The amounts, contracts and accounts are not public.** There are no statements, invoices or audit materials that can be checked for the claims of unpaid bills or misappropriated revenue. Reports also differ in the spelling of the company and representative names.
- **Do not treat social-media actions as evidence.** Unfollowing, deleting posts, going silent or pausing updates do not by themselves prove who is lying; equally, continuing to post is not an admission or a denial.
- **Respect privacy.** Koyoi's spouse is not a public party to this dispute, so this page does not name him. For the company, it keeps only the name used in public reporting and notes the spelling variation.
- **Separate this dispute from other events.** The health-related incident at a Taiwan event in 2024 and the February 2025 retirement decision have no direct causal link to the money dispute and should not be folded into the same timeline.

## Aftermath

From October 2025 onward, ami opened a new fan club managed by herself and continued updating it in 2026. Nanase has continued her activities, and Koyoi's YouTube, DJ work and official fan club also remain active. As of October 2026, this page has found no public report of a settlement, formal apology or court ruling, and the legal status of the dispute remains unclear.

For the industry, the case is often cited as an example of the problem of fan-club and merchandise licensing: when a performer outsources operations to a small company, who controls the contract, the receiving account, the rights to the material and the sales records matters more than verbal trust. Whatever the eventual finding on responsibility, that point goes beyond a personal quarrel between two actresses.
