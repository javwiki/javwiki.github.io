# AV actresses and overseas “work”

Working overseas ordinarily means taking a job abroad. In some Japanese reporting, however, “kaigai dekasegi,” and its counterparts in Chinese online discussion, serve as euphemisms for providing paid sexual services overseas. This article examines that usage and its intersection with the AV industry, distinguishing public commercial appearances, private transactions, and coerced sexual exploitation. Occupation alone does not establish an individual's conduct.

## Different activities under one label

| Activity | What needs verification | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expos, autograph sessions, photo sessions, and brand partnerships | Organizer, public schedule, duties, and payment arrangements | Overseas appearances do not establish paid sexual services |
| Overseas filming or content production | Producer, performance and distribution permissions, local law, and work authorization | Japanese performance experience does not guarantee overseas compliance |
| Private companionship or paid sexual services | Actual services, parties, intermediaries, and applicable rules | Labels such as “dinner” or “travel” do not establish the actual content |
| Coercion and exploitation disguised as work | Deception, threats, debt, and control of documents or movement | Agreeing to travel does not mean agreeing to every subsequent demand |

For example, JKFace's 2025 TRE page lists a public event at Taipei Nangang Exhibition Center on **8–10 August**, along with its actress lineup. The schedule documents event arrangements, not private sexual transactions. [JKFace: 2025 TRE event information](https://jkface.net/events/179)

## Connections with the AV industry

AV releases can create recognition abroad, but how recognition becomes income requires individual verification. Public events sell agreed activities such as meetings, autographs, or photography. Private intermediaries may market an “AV actress” identity as a reason for higher prices. Publicity may also misuse stage names or photographs, so a list alone cannot establish involvement.

In a column dated **29 April 2023**, former actress たかなし亜妖 described the overseas-work discussion of the time as referring to paid sexual services across borders, including work at establishments and private clients arranged by intermediaries. This is the author's industry commentary, not a participant register or population survey. [Nikkan SPA!: overseas-work column](https://nikkan-spa.jp/1908380)

Seeking more income, fewer filming jobs, and repayment pressure can describe different people's circumstances. In a discussion published on **6 March 2024**, Aki Sasaki recounted an acquaintance in nonexclusive AV work choosing overseas work after struggling to obtain bookings. This secondhand individual account cannot establish an industry-wide pattern or, on its own, show that the AV performer protection law caused an increase in overseas sexual transactions. [Nikkan SPA!: performer discussion](https://nikkan-spa.jp/1981920)

## Recruitment and intermediaries: who controls the terms?

Cross-border arrangements require distinguishing recruiters, referrers, local operators, and payers. Who promised the work, determined services and prices, and collected payment? Could the worker refuse a client or leave early? These are questions for investigation, not a claim that every project follows the same organizational chain.

Debt can change the conditions of an apparently free choice. Japan's Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare documents cases of prostitution to repay debts from host-club spending. This supports examining debt pressure but does not show that every actress working abroad has a connection with host clubs. [MHLW: measures against abusive host clubs](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/stf/seisakunitsuite/bunya/kodomo/kodomo_kosodate/dv/index_00037.html)

AV management contracts also need to be distinguished from separately introduced overseas work. Having an agency does not mean it handles every private invitation. Attributing a transaction to a particular company requires independent evidence.

## Reading claims of large earnings in a short period

A book excerpt published by Bunshun Online on **16 May 2025** reports a contact's claims about high earnings in Taiwan and links the highest payments to local name recognition. Such anonymous claims provide neither a representative sample nor individual settlement records, and cannot establish average overseas earnings for actresses. [Bunshun Online: a contact's account of the Taiwan market](https://bunshun.jp/articles/-/78965?page=3)

At minimum, high-payment reports require these distinctions:

- Is the figure the client's total payment or the worker's share? What intermediary fees or additional deductions apply?
- Does it cover one booking, one day, or the whole trip? Are exceptional maximum earnings presented as typical?
- Who bears airfare, accommodation, waiting time, cancellations, medical expenses, and taxes?
- Did a transaction occur at the quoted price, and was the promised money paid?

These are accounting questions, not grounds for inventing a take-home percentage without records. A large yen figure converted from foreign currency does not imply equivalent workloads, costs, or purchasing power.

## Entry status, legality of services, and coercive control

Entry and work authorization, the legality of actual services, and exploitation require separate assessment. For example, the US Department of State states that B1/B2 visitors may not accept employment or work in the United States. This rule cannot simply be applied to every country or business activity. [US Department of State: visitor visas](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/tourism-visit/visitor.html)

A jurisdiction's treatment of sexual services does not automatically authorize foreign visitors to provide them. A specific case requires checking local rules at the relevant time and the actual conduct. Additional entry questioning or refusal of admission does not itself establish involvement in sexual transactions.

Japan's National Police Agency lists forced prostitution to repay debt and forced labor following passport confiscation as examples of trafficking victimization. Coercion and control are central: voluntarily traveling or having performed in AV does not rule out subsequent victimization. [National Police Agency: trafficking and examples of abuse](https://www.npa.go.jp/bureau/safetylife/hoan/jinshintorihiki/index.html)

## Visa refusals and difficulties at the border

Reports of actresses being unable to travel abroad require identifying the stage involved. Online references to a “visa refusal” sometimes conflate these different outcomes:

| Situation | Stage | What needs confirmation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visa application refused | Embassy or consulate adjudication | Reason, legal provision, and whether further documents were requested |
| Admission refused and traveler sent back | Border inspection after arrival | Admission decision and reasons; this is distinct from consular visa refusal |
| Further questioning or a lengthy wait | Additional border checks | Whether admission was ultimately granted or refused, rather than waiting time alone |

The US Department of State explains that consular officers adjudicate visas. Missing information or failure to qualify for the requested category can lead to refusal; occupation alone does not establish the reason. [US Department of State: visa denials](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html) A visa also does not guarantee admission, which Customs and Border Protection (CBP) determines on arrival. [US Department of State: visas and admission](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/frequently-asked-questions.html)

### Yua Fukuda: her account of refusal of entry to New Zealand

In an interview published on **17 August 2026**, Yua Fukuda said she traveled to New Zealand to see the stars but was sent back after nearly six hours of questioning. According to her account, the document she signed cited an unclear travel purpose that could not be accepted as tourism. [Nikkan SPA!: interview with Yua Fukuda](https://nikkan-spa.jp/2180698)

This is a personal account, not a complete case file released by immigration authorities. It is included as an individual border incident, without establishing that she engaged in or intended sexual transactions. The reporting's term for forced return also does not, by itself, establish a particular legal procedure.

### Mana Sakura: released after lengthy questioning

ORICON reported on **25 June 2026** that Mana Sakura recalled visiting the United States for a photo book shoot at age 23, being held for about five hours, and being released after presenting the shooting schedule. This was a recollection, not a visa refusal occurring in 2026. [ORICON: Mana Sakura's televised account](https://www.oricon.co.jp/news/2463777/full/)

The publication date, timing of the incident, and final outcome must remain distinct. Her speculation about the background to the questioning cannot be treated as the border authority's confirmed reason.

### Occupational labels and applicable rules

Immigration New Zealand's operational manual E7.40 prohibits temporary entry class visa holders from providing commercial sexual services, operating a prostitution business, or investing in one while in New Zealand; breaches may lead to liability for deportation. These activity restrictions do not establish a blanket ban on AV actresses entering as tourists. [Immigration New Zealand: E7.40](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/opsmanual/34359.htm)

Assessing whether occupation caused a refusal requires the application category, travel purpose, submitted evidence, and decision reasons. These cases and rules do not establish that every country bans AV actresses from entering, nor do they provide visa-refusal or admission-refusal rates for actresses as a group.

## What the reporting establishes and what remains unknown

These sources document terminology, some people's experiences or observations, and institutional issues. They do not supply a population sample from which to estimate participation among active or retired AV actresses. Japanese women, sex-industry workers, AV actresses, and identified trafficking victims are different statistical populations; their counts are not interchangeable.

Public schedules, overseas photographs, displays of wealth, and intermediaries' “rosters” cannot independently confirm participation. Case reporting must distinguish personal accounts, media retellings, police allegations, and court findings, while retaining publication dates and subsequent corrections. Discussion should examine verifiable working relationships, money, and controlling conduct instead of attaching names to rumors.
