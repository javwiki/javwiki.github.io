# The “forced retirement” controversy around Akari Mitani

Akari Mitani (now performing as Akane Mitani; 美谷朱音) announced in July 2024 that she would retire from AV on 19 July 2025. Chinese-language online discussions then spread a claim: she did not choose to retire; she was jointly blacklisted by eight agencies, her co-appearances were cut, and she was forced out. The claim is widespread, but “forced retirement” is a conclusion, not a confirmed fact. This article separates the timeline, the origin of the claim, her own statements and the verifiable evidence, and sets out what can and cannot be established.

## Timeline: from Akari Mitani to Akane Mitani

| Date | Event | Nature |
| --- | --- | --- |
| July 2017 | AV debut as Akari Mitani | Public career record |
| 13 January 2023 | Announced transfer to Allpro | Her X post |
| 17 November 2023 | Transferred to LiStar; renamed 美谷朱音 (Akane Mitani) | Her X post |
| 5 July 2024 | Announced retirement on 19 July 2025 | Her X post |
| 22 April 2025 | Final title completed | Her X post |
| 19 July 2025 | Retired from AV | Public reports |
| 24 November 2025 | Debuted as a 63ANGEL dancer | 63ANGEL announcement |

The timeline does not show a sudden disappearance: she announced about a year in advance, completed a final title, held a farewell, and moved to the stage. That looks like a planned career change. But it only shows that the retirement was arranged in advance; it does not prove she was under no pressure. A “plan” can also be made under pressure.

## Where the “forced retirement” claim comes from

A long Chinese-language article claims that her agency, Allpro, clashed with CRUSE GROUP over poaching; eight agencies jointly boycotted Allpro and refused to appear with its actresses; Mitani’s co-appearance work was affected and her work was restricted, so she had no choice but to retire. The article also mentions other actresses’ transfers and third-party mediation. [NetEase: Mitani Akane’s retirement and the eight-agency boycott](https://www.163.com/dy/article/J6JIB0TQ0553XLZG.html)

The claim comes mainly from Chinese-language self-media and a Taiwanese AV commentator’s account. This article found no corresponding report in Japanese mainstream media, and no public confirmation from an agency, industry body or the person herself. The number “eight” has no verifiable list or document; the scope, duration and method of the alleged boycott are not public.

Notably, the self-media article itself says it cannot determine whether the boycott was the main reason for her retirement; it leans towards the view that she had reached a stage where she wanted to retire, and that the boycott may have been one trigger. In other words, even the source spreading the claim does not treat “forced retirement” as established fact.

## What she herself says

In a December 2025 interview with Nikkan SPA!, she explained her reason for retiring. She said she had no “sales persona”, felt she had done everything she could as her true self, and had reached her ceiling. She wanted to announce a year in advance and say goodbye to fans properly, and thought that was a good way to close the curtain. [Nikkan SPA!: Akane Mitani on why she retired](https://nikkan-spa.jp/2132401)

In April 2025, she said on X that her final title was finished: “I’m glad I became an AV actress; I’m happy I could make a proper ending.” [Akari Mitani on X: final title completed](https://x.com/akari_mitani17/status/1914549700746993997)

This is her public account, not the whole inside story. But at least “forced” is not the reason she gave. She may withhold details for privacy, contract or industry reasons, but replacing her silence or her explanation with “forced retirement” means the reader is supplying the causation.

## What can and cannot be confirmed

Confirmed: she did transfer twice (CRUSE GROUP → Allpro → LiStar) and change her name; she announced retirement in July 2024 and retired in July 2025; she moved to 63ANGEL; the industry does have transfers, poaching, co-appearance restrictions and similar competitive tactics.

Not confirmed: the list of eight agencies and their joint action; whether the boycott existed; whether it targeted her personally; whether the boycott caused her retirement; whether she was pressured; the details of the agency conflict. There are no public contracts, industry-body documents, police or court records, or agency statements supporting the full “eight-agency boycott” story; “eight” may be a simplification of industry rumour.

Even if a boycott existed, it does not equal “forced retirement”. At the time she was a double exclusive for Honnaka and Das!, and solo titles and her exclusive contracts could continue. A co-appearance restriction may affect some work, but it is not enough on its own to explain retirement. Turning “fewer co-appearances” into “unable to work” skips too many steps.

## Why the “forced retirement” story is attractive

It offers a simple causal story: a popular actress sacrificed to an industry power struggle. It is more dramatic than “she felt she had reached her ceiling”, and easier to spread.

It also fits fans’ imagination of a “dark industry”. Transfers, poaching and co-appearance restrictions do occur, but the inside is opaque, so rumour fills the gap. Self-media has an incentive to attract traffic; compressing a complex conflict into “eight agencies versus one” and a personal choice into “forced” is the easiest way to spread.

This does not mean the claim is necessarily false. It means drama should not be treated as evidence. The tidier the story, the more its origin needs checking.

## Industry context: transfers, poaching and co-appearance restrictions

An AV actress’s contract with her agency, the difference between exclusive and kikaku-tantai work, and the casting of co-appearance titles can all be affected by relationships between agencies. Transfers are not rare; poaching, refusing co-appearances and restricting events are possible tactics.

But their effect depends on the actress’s tier, contract type and relationships with studios. For a double-exclusive actress centred on solo titles, a co-appearance restriction may affect some titles and events without cutting off all income. There is no public industry rule or list for an “eight-agency boycott”; an individual rumour cannot be treated as an industry-wide mechanism.

A more reasonable question is: if a restriction existed, which work did it affect, for how long, who enforced it, and could she avoid it by changing agency or studio? Without public answers, the conclusion “forced retirement” remains suspended.

## After the event

She completed her final title as planned and retired on 19 July 2025. On 24 November 2025 she debuted as a 63ANGEL dancer. In the December 2025 interview she also revealed that she had restored her real birthday, which had been changed for her AV debut. Retirement was not “disappearance” but a career change; she continues to work on stage, at events and with fans. [63ANGEL announcement](https://x.com/burlesque_rpg/status/1980258976647598275), [63ANGEL profile](https://rokusanangel.jp/talent/akanemitani/)

If a boycott had made work impossible, why could she complete a final title, hold a farewell and move to a major stage? This does not disprove the boycott claim, but it demands a more specific explanation.

## Common misconceptions

- **“The eight-agency boycott is a fact.”** At present it is only a Chinese-language self-media and commentator account; there is no list, document or Japanese mainstream confirmation.
- **“She was forced to retire.”** She describes it as her choice after reaching her ceiling. A boycott may have been a factor, but causation is not confirmed.
- **“A transfer means a boycott.”** Transfers are common; two transfers show instability, not a proven full boycott.
- **“If she does not explain, she is confirming it.”** Privacy, contracts and industry relationships can limit what she says; silence is not confirmation.
- **“A long self-media article is evidence.”** It can be a lead, but not a substitute for primary sources: contracts, announcements, the person or verifiable reporting.
- **“Retirement means disappearance.”** She moved to 63ANGEL and is active on stage.
- **“A high ranking means no boycott.”** Rankings measure residual popularity and title sales, not co-appearance opportunities, event bookings or industry relationships.

## What the sources show

What this article can confirm: her career timeline, two transfers and a name change, the date of her retirement announcement, the completion of her final title, her 63ANGEL debut, and her own public explanation of why she retired. It can also confirm that a claim of an “eight-agency boycott causing retirement” exists in Chinese-language online discussion, mainly from self-media and a commentator.

What it cannot confirm: the list of eight agencies and their joint action, whether the boycott existed, whether it targeted her, whether it caused her retirement, whether she was pressured, or the details of the agency conflict. There are no public contracts, industry-body documents, police or court records, or agency statements.

The biases are obvious: self-media has an incentive for traffic; fans have a demand for a “dark industry” story; her public account may be polished; agencies have no duty to publish internal contracts. Treating “a rumour we saw” as “a confirmed event” overstates the reliability of self-media and understates the agency of the person concerned.

As of the source-check date, **17 November 2026**, no Japanese mainstream report confirming the “eight-agency boycott” and no public statement from her or the agencies about the “eight-agency boycott” was found. “Forced retirement” therefore remains a claim awaiting verification, not a fact that can be written as established.
