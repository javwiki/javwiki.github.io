# Restrictions on overseas access to Japanese websites：current policies and contributing factors

Overseas availability depends on the platform, service, product and destination. Accessing a page, registering, paying and using content are separate conditions. This feature focuses on Japanese content platforms and draws on cross-border commerce guidance to explain contributing factors. Individual policies do not establish a rule for all Japanese websites.

Checked on 10 October 2026. Platform policies below come from official help pages. The discussion of factors explains general mechanisms; it does not establish a particular platform's reason unless the source explicitly states it.

## Current policies vary by service

| Service | Official overseas policy | Scope of the statement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FANZA | Intended for Japan; unavailable outside Japan | [Official help](https://support.dmm.co.jp/accounts/article/45804) establishes the service area but does not detail the reasons. It cannot establish a particular law or licensing contract as the cause. |
| DMM TV | Unavailable outside Japan | [Official help](https://support.dmm.com/dmm-tv/article/47591) describes this video service, rather than a policy for the entire DMM group. |
| TVer | Video viewing is limited to Japan; VPN viewing is unsupported | [Official help](https://help.tver.jp/hc/ja/articles/5106803908633-海外でも動画を視聴できますか) addresses viewing, rather than establishing that every webpage is inaccessible overseas. |
| DMM Online Salon | Overseas use is available with regional exceptions | [Official help](https://support.dmm.com/lounge/article/44577) lists regional and browser-language exceptions and supported foreign-issued cards. Forms and email offer English support, while the app has Japanese-language limitations. |

Video and community services can therefore adopt different overseas policies. This feature is not a survey of all Japanese sites and cannot estimate the proportion restricting overseas use.

## Where restrictions occur

| Stage | Possible restriction | Distinction to make |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page access | Region detection or network/proxy connections prevent access | A platform refusal, local network unavailability and a technical fault are different problems. |
| Registration and identity checks | Limited support for phone numbers, addresses or identity documents | An accessible page does not establish eligibility; check the particular service. |
| Payment | Unsupported methods, issuing regions, authentication or risk decisions | A card's network brand alone does not establish acceptance. |
| Content use | Regional restrictions on playback, downloads or individual titles | Payment eligibility and content rights require separate checks. |
| Delivery and support | Limited delivery areas, import eligibility, returns or customer support | Physical delivery and digital access follow different rules. |

This is a checking framework, not a claim that every platform applies every restriction. [DMM's region-error explanation](https://support.dmm.com/accounts/article/45604) notes that foreign providers, VPNs or proxies can cause connections within Japan to be classified as overseas. An error alone establishes neither the user's actual location nor the reason for the policy.

## Contributing factors

### Content rights and distribution territories

Rights may be divided among distributors and licensed by territory. Japanese rights do not automatically include other markets. [Netflix's licensing explanation](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/125345) describes unavailable regional rights and exclusivity elsewhere. This illustrates the mechanism; it does not establish FANZA's, TVer's or DMM TV's contract terms.

### Destination-country rules and age checks

Being based in Japan does not mean only Japanese rules matter. [UK regulator Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-rules-what-you-need-to-know) explains that overseas pornography services with qualifying UK links can fall within its rules and require effective age checks. Its [March 2026 industry bulletin](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-industry-bulletins/online-safety-industry-bulletin-march-2026) reported that seven of the top 100 dedicated pornography services had blocked UK users as of the end of January. This is an example of blocking in a regulatory environment, not a statistic about Japanese platforms.

### Payment channels, authentication and fraud controls

Legal businesses are not automatically eligible for payment processing. [Stripe's prohibited and restricted business rules](https://stripe.com/jp/legal/restricted-businesses) prohibit pornography and related services. This illustrates a separate payment constraint; it does not imply that FANZA uses Stripe. [Stripe's explanation of Japan's 3D Secure requirements](https://support.stripe.com/questions/3ds-mandate-in-japan?locale=ja-JP) describes guidelines requiring Japanese online merchants to implement authentication by the end of March 2025, including those accepting overseas online payments. Implementation adds work; the requirement does not prohibit foreign cards.

### Data processing and overseas outsourcing

Sending personal data to overseas support teams, cloud providers or partners may involve cross-border transfer obligations. [Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission Q&A](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/faq/APPI_QA/) explains that transfers to foreign third parties, including outsourcing, generally require the relevant consent or an applicable exception. A foreign user accessing a Japanese website is not automatically such a transfer.

### Logistics, taxes and localization costs

[JETRO's 2026 cross-border commerce guide](https://www.jetro.go.jp/ext_images/services/amazon_japan_store/ekkyoechandbook.pdf) identifies international shipping, taxes, import rules, multilingual support and payment localization as operational burdens. Physical commerce needs customs and delivery arrangements; digital services still require translation, support and systems investment. Limited expected overseas revenue may lead a business to restrict its markets. That is a general inference from costs, not a verified account of a particular company's decision.

## Effects on users, businesses and research

The following are possible consequences of the mechanisms above, rather than quantitative research findings.

- **Overseas users may encounter an interrupted process.** Accessible product information may lead to unavailable registration, purchasing or playback, increasing the work of checking eligibility. A translated page does not establish transaction support.
- **Market expansion requires additional investment.** Rights, payments, support and compliance can increase both revenue opportunities and fixed costs. Opening one territory does not mean worldwide availability.
- **Research and historical records may become harder to access.** Restrictions can prevent overseas researchers from reading product information or official rankings, making third-party archives useful. Their dates, ranking scopes and sources still need checking; they do not establish current official policies.

## Checking a particular website

Record the service, function, product, destination country or region, and check date. Review official overseas-use, payment, licensing, delivery and support explanations separately. Preserve specific source links and distinguish stated policy, observed access and inferred reasons. One failed visit does not establish a comprehensive block; one successful page load does not establish overseas service availability.
